# Sector rules to check

Advertising rules change and differ per country. This file says what to check, not the final answer. Always search for the current rules for the user's country and channel, and say when a specialist should review the text. You are not a lawyer.

## Always (all sectors)
- No misleading claims, no fake reviews or testimonials, no false scarcity or deadlines. In the EU this is covered by the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive; in the Netherlands also by the Dutch Advertising Code (Nederlandse Reclame Code, Stichting Reclame Code).
- Platform ad policies apply on top of the law (special ad categories for credit, housing, employment, health and politics on several platforms).
- Comparative advertising must be fair, factual and verifiable.

## Health, food supplements, wellness
- Never claim a product prevents, treats or cures a disease or a mental health condition.
- EU: nutrition and health claims on foods and supplements must follow Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006 and the EU register of permitted claims; wording must match an authorised claim.
- Medicines and medical devices have their own advertising rules. Netherlands: health product advertising is pre-checked by the Keuringsraad.
- Care providers: no patient stories or before-and-after results without explicit consent; never health data.

## Finance
- Risk warnings and balanced information for credit, investments and crypto. Netherlands: supervised by the AFM, including rules for financial influencers.
- No guaranteed returns, no "get rich" framing.

## Children
- No direct calls to children to buy or to pester parents. Extra rules for food high in fat, sugar or salt aimed at children.

## Alcohol
- Netherlands: Advertising Code for Alcoholic Beverages (STIVA). No appeal to minors, no link with performance, driving or social success.

## Gambling
- Netherlands: Kansspelautoriteit (Ksa) rules for licensed providers, strict limits on untargeted advertising and on role models.

## Legal and professional services
- Professional bodies may restrict claims (for example about results or specialisations). Check the relevant professional code.

## How to respond
1. Name the rule that likely applies in one sentence.
2. Refuse wording that is plainly not allowed, and say why.
3. Offer compliant wording that keeps the persuasive strength (experience, process, real customer outcomes within the rules).
4. Recommend a specialist check for anything regulated before publishing.
