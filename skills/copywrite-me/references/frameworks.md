# Frameworks

## Awareness levels (Eugene Schwartz)

| Level | Reader knows | Lead with |
|-------|--------------|-----------|
| Unaware | Nothing yet | A story, a surprising fact, an identity they recognise |
| Problem aware | The problem | The problem in their words, then the cost of leaving it |
| Solution aware | Solutions exist | Why this kind of solution, your mechanism |
| Product aware | You exist | Proof, the offer, risk reversal, objections |
| Most aware | Ready to buy | The offer and the call to action, short |

The less aware the reader, the longer and more story-driven the text.

## Structures

- **PAS:** Problem, Agitate (the cost), Solution. Good for problem-aware readers and short formats.
- **AIDA:** Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. General purpose.
- **BAB:** Before, After, Bridge. Good for transformation offers and posts.
- **Long-form sales page:** opening (headline, subheadline, lead), problem and dream, reframe and mechanism, offer, proof, objections and FAQ, close.
- **Story post:** hook, tension, turn, lesson, soft call to action.
- **Carousel:** slide 1 hook, slides 2 to n one idea each, last slide action. Each slide must work on its own.

## Headlines and hooks: the 4 U's

Useful, Urgent (only if true), Unique, Ultra-specific. Score each variant on the four; keep the best two for an A/B test.

Hook patterns that respect the reader: a specific number, a sharp contrast (old way, new way), a question they are already asking, a short confession, a counterintuitive claim you can prove.

## Editing pass

1. Cut the first paragraph if the text still works without it.
2. One idea per sentence, one point per paragraph.
3. Replace abstractions with examples and numbers.
4. Replace "we" sentences with "you" sentences where it helps.
5. Read it aloud; fix where you stumble.
6. Check every claim against the proof list.
